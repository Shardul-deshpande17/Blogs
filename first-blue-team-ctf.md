# My First Blue Team CTF: What I Learned and What's Next

I recently participated in TryHackMe's **"It's Downtime!" Blue Team CTF**, and it was my first dedicated CTF focused on defensive security. I was genuinely excited to take part. My goal wasn't simply to finish every challenge or collect every flag. I wanted hands-on experience, an opportunity to explore unfamiliar areas of cybersecurity, and a better understanding of where my skills currently stand.

## A Gentle Start

The first few exercises were a great warm-up. They were enjoyable, felt approachable, and gave me a confidence boost. At that point, I felt like I was getting into the flow of things and becoming more comfortable with the event.

## The Reality Check

Then came the five CTF challenges, and the difficulty increased considerably. They showed me how much I still have to learn. Some investigation steps were unfamiliar, and I got stuck more than once.

I didn't figure out every step independently. When I encountered difficulties, I referred to additional learning resources and references to help me understand the evidence and work through unfamiliar parts of the investigations. I treated these moments as opportunities to learn.

It's also worth separating two things: how hard I found the five CTF challenges is different from how I did in the event's other activities. The challenges are where most of my learning happened.

## What I Learned

Throughout the challenges, I encountered several techniques that were new to me, including handling disk images, extracting embedded files, working with encrypted artifacts and hashes, using command-line tools, and exploring digital forensics.

I wouldn't say I've mastered these techniques. Following an investigation and understanding its steps is different from being able to perform the entire process confidently on my own. Understanding a concept is one thing; working through real evidence and deciding what to look at next is another. The experience gave me a clearer picture of what I need to work on, and identifying those gaps is an important first step toward improving them.

## What Comes Next

My next goal is to revisit the techniques I struggled with and practise them independently, without rushing straight to an answer. I want to understand what each command does, why a particular approach is used, and what the evidence actually tells me.

Eventually, I'd like to build my own investigation playbook: a personal reference documenting the techniques I've learned, useful commands, and the reasoning behind my investigations. That way, I can approach similar challenges with a stronger foundation instead of starting from scratch every time.

## Final Thoughts

My first dedicated Blue Team CTF was exciting, challenging, and humbling. Not everything went smoothly, but that was part of the experience.

I came away with new knowledge, a clearer understanding of my current limitations, and plenty of motivation to practise. I joined to gain experience, and the event gave me exactly that: a better idea of where I stand and what I need to do next.

I'm glad I participated, and I'm looking forward to returning to these challenges with more experience and a stronger practical foundation.
